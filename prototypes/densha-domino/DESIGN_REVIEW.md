# Approved visual and game-feel pass — 19 November 2026

The owner approved this pass with “go ahead with the sushi changes and go live when done”. Implemented on the permanent sushi branch, preserving puzzle rules and the separate Claude edition. The points below record the approved targets; publication and measured results are recorded in BUILD_STATUS.md and the release provenance.

- **Pillars:** regenerate warm ivory restaurant/service-counter columns as decorative blockers on existing blocked board cells. A clearly solid base occupies one cell; fade only the upper shaft to imply height. Remove the outside-board background columns. Preserve puzzle occupancy and solutions.
- **Dead-end tracks:** a low, flush ivory conveyor stop cap with a muted plum rubber strip at the existing blocked boundary. No extra clutter at crossings and no stop cap at tunnel exits. Do not introduce a new collider or block a currently usable tile.
- **Train readability:** first compare a 1.5–2 logical-pixel warm-plum outer silhouette against the same board with quieter static contrast. Keep restaurant detail; reduce competing floor/track/prop contrast selectively. Do not outline every sushi grain/window seam or add black strokes everywhere. Bake outlines once into art/runtime textures rather than adding per-car postprocessing.
- **Size:** start with plates 15% larger and sushi 20% larger, with cab-specific placement preserving the two eyes, head stripe and light sockets. Check adjacent-lane clearance and all four tunnel mouths before integrating.
- **Food spring:** the current brake reaction produces about 3.75% vertical food scaling at its peak. Aim for a clearly legible 12–16% compression/rebound, anchored to the plate contact point. Keep train bodies grounded, plates seated, food motion subtle during cruising, and retain the carriage delay. Tune braking/startup separately from acknowledgement/honk.
- **Stars:** current stars use Elastic.Out for 567 ms with 140 ms stagger; the third star settles around 847 ms, while confetti starts at 280 ms. Replace this overlap with a short staged arrival/impact/settle, ascending chimes at each landing, and a short third-star hold before confetti. A starting design is roughly 120–150 ms arrival, one controlled 1.15–1.2 overshoot, 150–200 ms settle, and 140–180 ms between stars. Quad easing can shape arrival/settle; restrained elastic/back motion should be deliberate, not a generic wobble applied to every object.
- **Other UI:** short button compression and release; a restrained panel arrival. Reserve the strongest emphasis for awards rather than making all UI animate equally.
