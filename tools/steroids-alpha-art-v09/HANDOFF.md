## Implement first

1. Integrate the approved starter ship rig with existing client intent/physics state; preserve current server movement authority and networking. Do not copy lab key listeners or DOM controls into gameplay. Renderer reference: gpu-sprites.mjs (25 vertices/32 triangles per part; parity tested against approved projectPanelPoint). Weapon recoil is documented/tested in the frozen rig; the environment demo is not a replacement firing system.
2. Thruster rectangles: steady emission; one burst on demand transition, then sustained stronger emission; W drives both engines, yaw drives outside engine. Blue/white with some orange. Reference thrusters.mjs and tests. Derive nozzle origins after FFD. Tune/scale particles with the craft. Keep a minimum cruise visually distinct from throttle; the art-lab 180 to 810 px/s numbers are reference feel, NOT authority to silently rewrite network physics or balance. Put adopted values in tune.json.
3. Use selected composition C: small centred ship (18% of native lab calibration size), circular side rims visible, top/bottom planet outside the viewport. Native 1440x810 reference radius ~608.25. Repeating quiet terrain base, sand channels, readable medium props and occasional salvage. Main UI is a review tool, not production UX.
4. Keep hard ship shadow close, slightly offset below at initial daylight (~17px right/27px down in ground coordinates, plus craft hover lift). A broad Soft Light inner rim and soft 72px outer halo replace the thin bright outline. Port through Phaser renderer/pipeline; preserve visual result and avoid replacing current render architecture blindly.
5. Fix large-prop popping using footprint-aware culling and subdivided ground triangles clipped at the horizon with UV interpolation; fade the last 12px at the limb. Never reject a whole landmark because its centre or one corner is hidden. See surface-props.mjs, GPUSprites.groundSprite and progressive-area tests. Tall geometry will need its own occlusion model.
6. NEWEST decision: remove the independent sun cycle. lighting.mjs transforms one fixed sun (initial azimuth238/elevation60) into a local rolling-sphere frame using accumulated player travel PLUS planet rotation. planetFrame returns the SAME terrain camera and local sun; do not update one without the other. Rotation scrolls terrain by R*angle. Terrain light, rim brightness, sprite brightness, and ship shadow direction/length/opacity follow that frame together. Turning in place leaves sunlight unchanged. Shadows fade at night and length is bounded near the horizon. Rotate planet, phase slider and speed control are reference/debug controls only; synchronize actual world time/rotation consistently across clients, rather than separate per-client lighting timers.

## Limits and acceptance

This is a local repeating-map / rolling-sphere demonstration, not a globally continuous globe atlas. Full turns repeat the lighting while continuing through the tiled asset field. Preserve existing toroidal/server map logic; choose a deliberate gameplay mapping for world position and rotation. Do not ship the lab's latitude/longitude approximation as if global navigation were solved. Painted ship highlights/prop shadows remain baked; the new sprite pass only changes overall brightness. No claim of production FPS: automated Brave preview was slow despite low CPU submission. Profile foreground desktop AND phone; avoid per-frame texture uploads, shadow rebuilds or large allocations.
